MECHANICS — APPLY ONLY WHAT IS RELEVANT
- Establish the necessary FBD and equilibrium before stress calculations. Isolate the correct body/section; distinguish axial N, shear V, bending M and torsional T. Use perpendicular moment arms and consistent local axes. For CCW-positive planar moments use signs consistent with Mz=rx*Fy-ry*Fx. A negative result reverses an assumed arrow.
- For distributed loads, calculate the resultant and centroid on the selected segment. Recalculate intensity at a cut. A triangular resultant acts one-third of its base from the larger-intensity end. Use direct geometry when sufficient; no unused integrals.
- Use the actual solid, annular, net or built-up resisting area without double-counting overlaps. Distinguish average direct shear V/A from maximum beam shear stress. Determine actual shear planes and force per fastener; equal sharing needs justification. Check all specified failure modes and select the governing allowable load. Do not apply a safety factor twice to an allowable stress.
- Use tension-positive axial force/strain and compression-negative unless another convention is shown. Preserve signed displacement and final direction. Engineering strain uses original length: epsilon=(Lprime-L0)/L0. Engineering shear strain is the signed change from a right angle in radians; distinguish gamma from gamma/2.
- Combine equilibrium with compatibility for rigid bars and indeterminate systems. A rigid bar's small-rotation displacement field is linear. Equal endpoint displacement does not imply equal support forces. Include moving supports and actual stiffness AE/L. Wires carry tension only. Sum signed NL/(AE) contributions; integrate only for continuously varying N, A or E. Use exact deformed geometry where needed; mark small-angle approximations mathematically.
- Use sigma=E*epsilon and tau=G*gamma only in the applicable linear-elastic range. Read E from the initial straight-line graph slope. Select the correct tension/compression curve and loading segment; show interpolation when needed. Do not extrapolate the initial elastic line through plastic loading.
- Follow the given unloading law. If unloading is linear elastic with slope E, recovered strain=sigma_max/E; permanent strain=epsilon_total-sigma_max/E; permanent extension=permanent strain times original length. Distinguish total deformation, recovery and permanent deformation.
- For isotropic linear elasticity use epsilon_lateral=-nu*epsilon_axial and G=E/[2(1+nu)]. Diameter change=epsilon_lateral*d0; final diameter=d0+change only when requested. Include thermal effects, gaps or other assumptions only when required by the actual problem.
- Keep units consistent: 1 MPa=1 N/mm^2; 1 GPa=1000 N/mm^2; 1 kN=1000 N; 1 kip=1000 lbf; 1 ksi=1000 psi; 1 ft=12 in. Strain is dimensionless; angular strain/rotation uses radians unless another output unit is requested.
- For topics beyond the examples, use the appropriate standard mechanics model and necessary assumptions. Never claim the notes contain a nonexistent table or worked example.
