MANUALLY REVIEWED COURSE METHODS — SELECT ONLY WHAT THE CURRENT PROBLEM NEEDS

1. Equilibrium and sections (SOM_Tuto pp1–4)
Identify the support constraints and external forces. For an ideal planar pin use two force components; a planar roller supplies a force normal to its support; a fixed support can supply two forces and a couple; a cable supplies tension along itself. Do not infer support geometry from labels alone.
Use sum Fx=0, sum Fy=0, sum M=0 for planar statics, with actual perpendicular moment arms. For a cut use either isolated side and the necessary axial force N, shear V, bending moment M and torsional moment T. Keep a declared convention and reverse an assumed arrow when its solved scalar is negative.
For a selected distributed-load segment: R=integral w(x) dx; xbar=integral x*w(x) dx/R. Uniform-load resultant is w*L at L/2. Triangular resultant is wmax*L/2 at L/3 from the higher-intensity end. Recompute the intensity at the cut rather than using the full beam's resultant on a shorter free body. Direct geometry may replace unnecessary integrals.

2. Average stresses, area and connections (SOM_Tuto pp5–8,10)
Average axial stress sigma=N/A, where N is the section force from equilibrium. Use the appropriate sign or a tension/compression label. Average direct shear tau=V/As, with the actual shear plane(s). This is not a universal formula for maximum beam shear stress.
Solid circular area=pi*d^2/4; annulus=pi*(Do^2-Di^2)/4; rectangular area=b*t. For built-up sections avoid counting overlaps twice; subtract holes when the resisting net section requires it.
For fasteners identify the load in each fastener and number of resisting shear planes. If equal sharing is justified for n identical fasteners and k equivalent planes per fastener, average tau=P/(n*k*Af). Do not assume n or k from the fastener count alone.
For allowable load evaluate each specified failure mode and take the governing minimum. For sizing rearrange the actual force/area inequality, selecting a practicable dimension only when requested. If stress is already given as allowable, do not apply an additional unspecified safety factor.

3. Geometric strain (SOM_Tuto pp9,11,13,14)
Engineering normal strain epsilon=(Lprime-L0)/L0; use ORIGINAL length. Length can be evaluated from coordinates, Pythagoras or cosine rule: c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab*cos(theta). Distinguish force direction and displacement direction.
Engineering shear strain gamma_xy is the signed change in the original right angle between initially perpendicular material lines. State the axes/sign convention; a decrease of that angle is positive under the usual convention. Angles in radians are required for angular strain and small-angle relations. Do not confuse gamma_xy with tensor component epsilon_xy=gamma_xy/2.
Use actual deformed point coordinates/angles when finite geometry is needed. Small-angle approximation delta approximately r*theta must use theta in radians and must be appropriate; it is not automatically exact r*sin(theta).

4. Axial elasticity, compatibility and rigid members (1 (8).pdf pp1–6)
Linear axial elasticity sigma=E*epsilon; uniform elastic segment delta=N*L/(A*E). Sum signed segment deformations to obtain an endpoint displacement relative to the chosen fixed reference. For continuous variations delta=integral N(x)/[A(x)*E(x)] dx.
First determine each segment's internal force from equilibrium. The applied end force need not equal the internal force in every segment of a multiply loaded rod.
For a rigid bar the small vertical displacement field is linear: delta(x)=delta(x0)+(x-x0)*theta. Therefore displacement differences divided by their separations match the same small rotation. A horizontal bar has equal endpoint displacement; reactions are generally stiffness-dependent, not automatically equal.
For a statically indeterminate bar combine independent equilibrium equations with geometric compatibility and delta_i=N_i*L_i/(A_i*E_i). Distinguish a common rigid bar from independent flexible members. Wires are tension-only. If a wire attaches to a moving upper member, the lower-end displacement includes its upper-support motion plus wire extension; do not omit the support contribution.
For a rigid-member/wire system with exact geometric elongation, determine final length and configuration before using a small-rotation approximation.

5. Stress-strain diagrams and unloading (SOM_Tuto p12; 1 (8).pdf pp7–8)
Young's modulus is the slope of the initial linear elastic segment, E=Delta sigma/Delta epsilon. Read graph axis scales, material branch (tension/compression) and actual loading segment. Offset yield, proportional limit, ultimate tensile strength and fracture stress are distinct quantities; use whichever is requested.
For a linear segment between graph points (epsilon1,sigma1) and (epsilon2,sigma2), epsilon=epsilon1+(sigma-sigma1)*(epsilon2-epsilon1)/(sigma2-sigma1), provided sigma is in that segment. An initial Hooke-law slope cannot be extrapolated through a plastic portion merely because E is known.
If the given unloading law is linear elastic with initial slope E: recovered strain=sigma_max/E; permanent strain=epsilon_total-sigma_max/E; permanent extension=epsilon_permanent*L0. Distinguish loaded total deformation, elastic recovery and permanent deformation. Do not assume an unloading path not stated or supported by the course model.
For beam tilt with a tension member at one end and compression at the other, track signed endpoint displacements; use their difference and actual endpoint spacing, not the extension of only one member.

6. Poisson contraction, shear elasticity and rubber (1 (8).pdf pp1–2,9–10)
For isotropic linear elasticity epsilon_lateral=-nu*epsilon_axial and G=E/[2*(1+nu)]. Diameter change Delta d=epsilon_lateral*d0; final diameter dprime=d0+Delta d. Do not report dimensionless lateral strain as a length change.
For uniform elastic simple shear tau=G*gamma; under an appropriate small-angle approximation delta approximately gamma*h, where h is the separation of the shearing faces, not automatically the pad's loaded-area dimension. Use the actual shear resisting area and force per pad; a symmetric two-pad support can share load equally when justified by the geometry.
For a friction-limited load first solve body equilibrium and the impending-slip condition F=mu_s*N at the actual contact. Then compute the rubber force/stress and deformation. The supporting normal reaction can depend on the applied horizontal load through its moment arm.

8. Units and output checks
1 MPa=1 N/mm^2; 1 GPa=1000 N/mm^2; 1 kN=1000 N; 1 kip=1000 lbf; 1 ksi=1000 psi; 1 ft=12 in. Strain is dimensionless, often written mm/mm or in/in. Engineering shear strain and rotation use radians unless conversion is requested. NL/(AE) gives mm for N, mm, mm^2 and N/mm^2; it gives inches for lbf, in, in^2 and psi.
Show the formula, actual numbers, essential intermediate operations and the requested final calculation. Preserve sign and direction. Keep an F -> D -> S mathematical solution; use only relevant formulas, not all relations in this reference.
