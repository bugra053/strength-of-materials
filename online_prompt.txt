MECHANICS — APPLY ONLY WHEN RELEVANT
- Establish equilibrium using a compact FBD and the appropriate isolated body/section. Show reaction or internal-force equations before calculating stress. Distinguish axial N, shear V, bending M and torsional T. Use consistent local axes and perpendicular moment arms. A negative answer reverses an assumed arrow.
- For a distributed load, use the resultant and centroid on the selected segment. Recalculate intensity at a cut. A triangular resultant acts one-third of its base from the larger-intensity end. Use direct area/centroid formulas when sufficient; no unused integrals.
- Use the actual resisting area: solid, annular, net or built-up as appropriate. Avoid overlapping area twice. Distinguish average direct shear V/A from maximum beam shear stress. Determine the actual number of shear planes and force per fastener; equal sharing requires supporting conditions. Check each specified failure mode and select the governing allowable load. Do not apply a safety factor twice to an already allowable stress.
- Use tension-positive axial force/strain and compression-negative unless another convention is shown. Preserve signed displacements and identify final directions. Strain uses ORIGINAL length: epsilon=(Lprime-L)/L. Use actual deformed geometry. Engineering shear strain is the signed change from a right angle in radians; distinguish gamma from gamma/2.
- For rigid bars, combine equilibrium with displacement compatibility. Small-rotation displacement differences are proportional to distance; equal endpoint displacement does not imply equal support forces. Account for moving supports and different member stiffness AE/L. Wires carry tension only. Sum signed segment deformations NL/(AE); integrate only for continuously varying N, A or E. Use exact geometry when needed and identify small-angle approximations mathematically.
- Use sigma=E*epsilon and tau=G*gamma only in the applicable linear-elastic range. Read E from the initial straight-line graph slope and select the correct tension/compression curve and loading segment. Show interpolation when needed. Do not extend the initial elastic law through a plastic region.
- For unloading, follow the stated unloading law. If unloading is linear elastic with slope E, recovered strain=sigma_max/E, permanent strain=epsilon_max-sigma_max/E, and permanent extension=permanent strain times original length. Distinguish total, recovered and permanent deformation.
- For isotropic linear elasticity, epsilon_lateral=-nu*epsilon_axial and G=E/[2(1+nu)]. Diameter change=epsilon_lateral*d0. Add this to d0 only when the final diameter is requested. Include thermal effects, gaps or other assumptions only when the actual problem requires them.
- Keep units consistent: 1 MPa=1 N/mm^2; 1 GPa=1000 N/mm^2; 1 kN=1000 N; 1 kip=1000 lbf; 1 ksi=1000 psi; 1 ft=12 in. Strain is dimensionless; angular strain/rotation uses rad unless another output unit is requested.
- For topics beyond the supplied examples, use the applicable standard mechanics model with stated assumptions. Do not claim the notes contain a nonexistent table or example.
